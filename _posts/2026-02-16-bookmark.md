  <div class="synopsis">
    <p>Seyon has never seen her own eyes. She knows they are blue — the pale, clinical blue noted in medical records — but she has never watched light pass through them, never caught her reflection in a window. She reads the world through her fingertips: the raised dots of Braille, the weight shift of a couch cushion, the exact temperature of someone's skin. She prays not to escape reality, but to ask for strength — for herself and for the woman she loves.</p>

    <p>Soyu was once a bright, curious child who learned to become small. A violent father, a hard divorce, a body that betrayed her through puberty. She didn't know she was a woman until a dream gave her the word for what she'd always felt. She began hormone therapy in secret, quietly choosing a future that felt true. Then she moved in with her childhood friend — the blind girl she'd once defended in a sandbox — and everything she had been holding in silence began, slowly, to surface.</p>

    <p>They have known each other since they were children. They fell in love in their final year of high school — not all at once, but through the slow accumulation of living side by side. One night, Soyu broke down and told Seyon she was not the boy Seyon might think she was. Seyon reinterpreted her faith, choosing love over prejudice, and took Soyu's hands.</p>

    <p>Now university freshmen at Korea University, they are learning that the world does not soften just because you have someone to come home to. Soyu endured a first semester of whispered gossip and kept distance, earning her professors' respect through sheer sincerity. Seyon built a quiet reputation of her own while navigating the small, stubborn assumptions people make about blindness. Together, they protected a life made of coffee between classes, late-night conversations, and weekends spent discovering places that belong only to them.</p>

    <p>It is winter break now. Soyu's first work-study shift is done. The days stretch out ahead, unhurried and unscheduled, and they intend to fill them with the smallest possible joys. But first — there is a family dinner approaching, and certain words that have never been spoken aloud.</p>
